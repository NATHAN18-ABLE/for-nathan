Jongno 종로구

Palaces, hanok lanes and a market supper

Jongno has been the heart of Seoul for six hundred years. Start at the greatest palace of the Joseon kings, take tea in a hanok, wander 1920s hanok lanes and the antique street, and end with dinner in Korea's first permanent market.

Length
About 9 hours

The day

  1. 10:00

    Gyeongbokgung Palace

    The largest palace of the Joseon dynasty

    • Time here: 1 hr 30 min
    • Cost: Adults ₩3,000
    • Closed Tuesdays
    Details

    The first and largest palace of the Joseon kings. See the throne hall Geunjeongjeon and the Gyeonghoeru pavilion on its pond, and catch the gate guard ceremony in front of Gwanghwamun.

    Wear a hanbok and entry is free; rental shops line the streets nearby. The guard ceremony is usually at 10:00 and 14:00.

  2. Walk 10 min

    11:40

    Seochon Dagwabang

    Traditional tea and Korean sweets in a hanok courtyard

    • Time here: 50 min
    • Cost: ₩10,000–15,000
    • Closed Tuesdays
    Details

    A hanok café in Seochon, the old neighborhood west of the palace. Sit in the small courtyard garden with traditional tea and sweets made in house.

    Opens at 11:00.

  3. Line 3 to Jongno 3-ga, 2 stops

    13:00

    Ikseon-dong hanok lanes

    Cafés and restaurants inside 1920s hanok lanes

    • Time here: 1 hr 30 min
    • Cost: Lunch ₩15,000–25,000
    Details

    Narrow lanes of hanok built in the 1920s, now full of cafés, restaurants and small shops. Have lunch here and wander.

  4. Walk 10 min

    14:40

    Insa-dong

    Antique shops, galleries and crafts along one street

    • Time here: 2 hr
    • Cost: Free to browse
    Details

    The center of calligraphy and antiques since the Joseon era. Antique dealers, galleries and craft shops still line the street, with tea houses and hanok galleries in the lanes behind. A good place for souvenirs.

  5. Line 1, 1 stop to Jongno 5-ga · or walk 20 min

    17:00

    Gwangjang Market

    Mung bean pancakes and gimbap at the market stalls

    • Time here: 1 hr 30 min
    • Cost: ₩10,000–20,000
    • Check prices first
    Details

    Opened in 1905 as Korea's first permanent market, and still one of Seoul's liveliest. Share bindaetteok, mini gimbap and yukhoe at the food stalls, a little of everything.

Prices are rough ranges per person. Opening hours, prices and pop-ups change, so check before you go. Place photos were gathered by the team during research.
