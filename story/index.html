Two thousand years of Seoul

Prehistory

Thousands of years ago

It began on the Han River

Seoul's story begins on the banks of the Han, where the river gave water, food and a place to settle. At Amsa-dong, the remains of Neolithic pit houses still mark where people lived.

Where to see it

  • Amsa-dong Prehistoric SettlementGangdong

18 BCE

18 BCE – 475

The city was called Wiryeseong · Hanseong

Hanseong, capital of Baekje

The Baekje kingdom built its first capital here and ruled from it for nearly five hundred years. Its earthen walls and royal tombs still stand in Songpa.

Where to see it

  • Mongchontoseong and the Hanseong Baekje MuseumSongpa day course
  • Seokchon-dong Ancient TombsSongpa day course
  • PungnaptoseongSongpa

1394

1394 – 19th century

The city was called Hanyang

Hanyang, capital of Joseon

In 1394 King Taejo made Hanyang the capital of Joseon. Gyeongbokgung, the royal shrine Jongmyo and the city wall were built, shaping what is still Seoul's historic heart.

Where to see it

  • Gyeongbokgung PalaceJongno day course
  • Insa-dongJongno day course
  • Changdeokgung and JongmyoJongno
  • Bukchon Hanok VillageJongno

1897

1897 – 1945

The city was called Gyeongseong

A modern city, then colonial rule

Streetcars, electric light and Western buildings arrived in the late 1800s, and the Korean Empire was declared in 1897. From 1910 to 1945 Korea was under Japanese rule, the city was called Gyeongseong, and many fought for independence.

Where to see it

  • Deoksugung PalaceJung
  • Seodaemun Prison History HallSeodaemun

1945

1945 – 1953

The city was called Seoul

Liberation and the Korean War

Korea was liberated in 1945, and in 1948 the Republic of Korea was founded with Seoul as its capital. The war that began in 1950 left much of the city in ruins; after the fighting stopped in 1953, rebuilding began.

Where to see it

  • War Memorial of KoreaYongsan
  • Seoul Museum of HistoryJongno

1960s

1960s – 1988

The Miracle on the Han River

From the 1960s the economy grew at speed, and the riverbanks and Gangnam were built up. Subway Line 1 opened in 1974, and the 1988 Olympics introduced Seoul to the world.

Where to see it

  • Olympic ParkSongpa day course
  • GangnamGangnam day course
  • Yeouido Hangang ParkYeongdeungpo day course

2000s

2000s – today

Where tradition meets the new

Seoul has kept its past while building something new, as when the Cheonggyecheon stream was brought back to downtown in 2005. Today the city is where K-pop, drama, food, fashion and art meet visitors from everywhere.

Where to see it

  • CheonggyecheonJung day course
  • Seongsu-dongSeongdong day course
  • MyeongdongJung day course

A city where two thousand years are still alive. Meet the past in its palaces and alleys, and walk today's Seoul in its streets.
